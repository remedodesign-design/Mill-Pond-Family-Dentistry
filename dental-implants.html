What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Once it has healed and bonded with the bone, it holds a custom-made crown, bridge or denture firmly in place. The result looks, feels and works like a natural tooth, with nothing to take out at night and no need to file down the teeth beside it.

Implants are an option for people who have lost teeth to gum disease, decay or injury, or who were born without a tooth. If you are missing several teeth, implants can also support a bridge or a denture, so you can eat, speak and smile with confidence again.

How do dental implants work?

  1. Placement. A small titanium implant is placed into the jawbone where the tooth is missing.
  2. Healing. Over the next few months the implant bonds with the bone in a process called osseointegration, forming a strong, stable foundation.
  3. Extraction, if needed. A damaged or decayed tooth may need to be removed first, so the area can heal before the implant goes in.
  4. Your new tooth. An abutment and a custom crown are added, shaded to match your natural teeth, restoring both the look and the function of the tooth.

Treatment options

Types of dental implant treatment

Every implant plan is tailored to your oral health, your bone structure and your smile goals. These are the options your dentist may discuss with you.

  • Single tooth implant

    One implant and a custom crown replace a single missing tooth, restoring full function and a natural look without touching the teeth beside it.

  • Multiple missing teeth

    Several missing teeth can be replaced with an implant-supported bridge: a stable, permanent solution that does not need a removable appliance.

  • Full arch and implant-supported dentures

    If you are missing all of your teeth in an arch, implants can support a full bridge or a denture that stays firmly in place.

  • Sinus augmentation

    When the upper jaw lacks height, bone can be built up below the sinus to create the foundation an implant needs. This is arranged with a dental specialist.

  • Bone grafting and ridge rebuilding

    Where bone has been lost, a graft rebuilds the jaw so it can support an implant. We coordinate this with a specialist when your case calls for it.

  • Careful planning

    An exam and X-rays map your bone, gums and bite before any treatment begins, so your plan is precise and every step is explained.

If you have discomfort, swelling or sudden pain during or after treatment, call us right away and we will see you as soon as we can. If your child has lost a tooth through injury, we can assess the area and help you plan for the future.

Why implants

Advantages of dental implants

Many people choose implants as their long-term answer to tooth loss. Here is why.

  • A natural look

    Implants closely resemble your own teeth in appearance, feel and function, for a smile that looks like yours.

  • Clear speech

    Loose dentures can shift and change how you speak. Implants stay put, so you can talk with confidence.

  • A comfortable fit

    Removable options can rub and irritate over time. Implants become part of your mouth and feel like it.

  • Easier maintenance

    Implants do not rely on the neighbouring teeth for support, which makes cleaning simpler and protects your overall oral health.

  • Long-term durability

    Built to handle daily wear, implants can last for many years, and often decades, with proper care.

  • A healthier jawbone

    An implant stimulates the bone beneath it, helping to prevent the bone loss that follows tooth loss and to preserve the shape of your face.

Implants are one of the most reliable ways to replace one tooth or several. They give you a firm foundation for fixed teeth made to match your natural smile.

Is it right for you?

Who is a good candidate for dental implants?

Most healthy adults who are missing teeth can have dental implants. The best candidates have healthy gums, enough jawbone to support the implant, and good oral hygiene.

People who smoke, have uncontrolled diabetes or have lost a lot of bone may need extra treatment before surgery, such as a bone graft. At your consultation we assess your gums, bone and general health carefully, so we can tell you honestly whether implants are right for you and what it will take to give them the best chance of lasting. If you have lost just one tooth, a single implant may be an ideal, natural-looking solution.

You may be a good candidate if you

  • are missing one or more teeth
  • have healthy gums and enough jawbone
  • brush, floss and keep up with dental visits
  • want a fixed alternative to a bridge or denture

You may need extra care first if you

  • smoke or use tobacco
  • have diabetes that is not well controlled
  • have lost bone in the jaw
  • have active gum disease or decay

What affects the cost of dental implants?

The price of treatment depends on the number of teeth being replaced, the type of restoration (a crown, a bridge or a denture), and whether any preparation is needed first, such as an extraction or a bone graft. More complex cases involve more steps, so they cost more.

Because every mouth is different, we do not quote a price before your exam. After your consultation you will receive a written estimate that breaks down each stage, and nothing starts until you are comfortable with it. Questions

Dental implants: your questions answered

What are dental implants, and do you offer them?

Dental implants are permanent tooth replacements anchored in the jawbone. We offer implant consultations and treatment planning at our Hamilton Road office, and we coordinate with a dental specialist when a case calls for it.

Who is a good candidate for dental implants?

Most healthy adults with missing teeth and enough healthy bone qualify. We assess your gums, bone and general health at your consultation.

How long does an implant take?

Usually a few months from start to finish, because the implant needs time to bond with the bone before the final crown is fitted. Your dentist will give you a timeline for your case.

Does getting an implant hurt?

The area is numbed for the procedure, so you should not feel pain. Mild soreness for a few days afterwards is normal and is usually managed with over-the-counter pain relief.

How many teeth can implants replace?

Implants can replace one tooth, several teeth or a whole arch. Your dentist will recommend how many implants you need and which type of restoration suits you, whether that is a crown, a bridge or a denture.

Are dental implants expensive?

Implants can cost more up front than a bridge or a denture, but they last longer, so many people find them the better value over time. We give you a written estimate before any work begins.

Are dental implants safe, and how long do they last?

Yes. Implants are made of biocompatible titanium, which bonds naturally with your jawbone, and they have been studied extensively. With good home care and regular check-ups they can last for many years, and often for decades.

Is an implant covered by insurance?

Coverage varies by plan. We will send an estimate to your insurer electronically so you know what to expect before treatment.

Am I too old for a dental implant?

There is no upper age limit. Your general health and the condition of your gums and jawbone matter more than your age.

What if I don’t have enough jawbone?

We check your bone with X-rays at your consultation. If it needs support, we will explain the options, which may include a bone graft arranged with a dental specialist.

Can I get an implant if I smoke or have a health condition?

Often yes, but smoking and some conditions, such as uncontrolled diabetes, can slow healing. We will review your health history and tell you honestly what to expect.

How do I look after an implant?

The same way as your natural teeth: brush, clean between them and keep your regular check-ups. An implant cannot decay, but the gums around it still need healthy habits.

What happens at the first consultation?

We examine your mouth, take X-rays, listen to your goals and explain your options. You will leave with a clear plan and a written estimate, with no obligation to go ahead.
